# Component base, coherence and the existing product

Read this before deciding a product's component base and restyle depth, how its foundations relate, and how new screens fit what exists.

Floors: `CRAFT.md`. Departures from what users know: "Familiar and new" in `REASONING.md`. Brand presence: `register.md`. Announcing changes to users is separate.

## Inputs

- An inventory: screens, components, tokens, brand guidelines, libraries, each distinct treatment side by side.
- Each value's origin: this product's decision, or the base's or the model's default.
- Per layer (behaviour and accessibility, structure, appearance): what the base supplies, who maintains it.
- How often current users meet affected screens; whether one conceptual structure still holds; whether the new screen's job differs.
- Platform conventions and their strength; tokens or values written into components.

## Criteria and evidence

- **Layers:** behaviour and accessibility cost most to build; appearance costs least to change and carries identity (practitioner and vendor accounts; no comparative study).
- **Defaults:** weak evidence they pull products alike (one correlational study of shared libraries; one preprint its own constraint may explain). A kept default carries a reason, as a change does.
- **Restyle depth:** guidance agrees, untested: colour, typeface, shape and icon family carry identity; size, placement, behaviour, standard symbols, navigation and modality conventions carry familiarity. A starting map: the inventory shows where this product's identity lives and which conventions its users rely on. No tested limit says how far appearance may move.
- **Modify or separate:** one system's guidance: changing most of a component, the need it serves or its markup exceeds a modification; a large change becomes a separately named component, outside the base's updates. Recheck modifications for breakage and accessibility on updates.
- **Consistency:** sources disagree, neither with data. One side evolves gradually, tolerating minor inconsistencies until redesign; radical change needs almost no current users and a large expected gain, or a lost conceptual structure (expert opinion). The other finds consistency an unreliable guide: fit with users' work contexts can matter more than matching what exists (one essay). Name the side taken and the product facts behind it.
- **Inherited brand:** weak evidence. Check whether product use needs more steps, weights, small functional icons or contrast than the guidelines give; agree who owns each part.
- **Unmeasured:** the harmony claimed for foundation relations; staged against all-at-once change.

## Options space

- **Base per layer:** inherited, themed, owned.
- **Local change:** the base's extension points, a marked modification, a separate component; local or offered back (untested).
- **Platforms:** one cross-platform form, or each platform's conventions.
- **Fit per element:** follow, improve here, improve and spread.
- **Brand assets:** carried, translated, product-owned.
- **Relation per foundation pair:** none, shared unit or grid, matched property, scale derived from another, values generated from few inputs, ramp per colour family.
- **Staging:** at once or stepped; by tokens or migration; flag, internal use, share of accounts, one area first; which parts lead, by what criterion; both designs for a period; end date.
- **Record weight:** lean, or tiered as the system grows.

## Testing

- Per restyled component, list identity and familiarity changes; each familiarity change names its job.
- Real content on the main views touched; toggle old and new.
- Check each stated relation in place, alignment included.
- Trace what a token change reaches; while both designs run, test each.
- Catch drift: consistency reviews, a periodic fresh inventory.

## Write down

Per layer, base and owner; per value, origin and reason; per restyled component, changes and reasons; local components, modified or separate; per new screen, what follows and departs, accepted inconsistencies and when they close; brand assets' fate and owner; options, then decisions in context; relations as references or derivations that later screens extend; staging steps and checks; drift check; evidence strength.
