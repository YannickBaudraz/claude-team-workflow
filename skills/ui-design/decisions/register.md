# Expressive register and brand presence

Read this before deciding how expressive a direction is, how much brand it shows, where the mark sits, and how far it follows its category.

`character.md` sets the character spent here; floors are in `CRAFT.md`, hue in `colour.md`, typefaces in `type.md`.

## Inputs

- Per screen class: is the user completing a job through inputs over long stays, judged by time and abandonment, or reading and exploring pages?
- The mix of newcomers, occasional and all-day users; the cost of a mistake; whether content brings its own colour and imagery; platform conventions.
- Whether users need telling where they are: several products, a switcher, shared or customer-branded screens.
- The brand's elements, and which users recognise as its alone (measured, or assumed).
- Which products form the category: no source says how to choose; name them as an assumption.

## Options space

- **Register:** one default for the product, or one per screen class.
- **Carriers:** colour, type, shape, containment, motion and interaction feedback, imagery, copy, each quiet or loud on its own.
- **Moments** that depart from the default, above or below it: which, through which carriers, how many.
- **Brand elements:** the roles of each brand colour (main action, status, selected state, controls, content), the reach of any brand typeface, voice, behaviour.
- **Mark:** task screens, shell or entry surfaces; size and states.
- **Category codes:** typical or departing, one by one.

## Criteria

- Each moment: does it matter emotionally, is it a key interaction, and what does it compete with where it sits (dense containers, the task in progress)? Where expression lives is disputed (below). Does the register hold within each task, component or region?
- Each brand element: what job does it do at small sizes, with accessibility settings, beside semantic colours? What space does a display-only element cost? Is it unique to the brand and widely recognised? The answers sort it: carried, adapted, or kept to marketing. Weak evidence: untested, drawn from advertising research.
- Do copy and behaviour keep the brand's stated claims?
- The mark: what context does it give? Where recall matters, does it sit where the reading direction expects (one controlled website test; in-app chrome untested)?
- Category codes: functional ones (placement, controls, symbols, terms) are weighed under "Familiar and new" in `REASONING.md`. Typicality and novelty both raise preference but suppress each other; how to balance them is untested. Costs of departing are better measured than gains: typical looks raise first-glance beauty ratings, and an unconventional logo place cut recall without looking more distinctive (one controlled study each); marketing research holds shared category styling cannot identify a brand.
- Evidence that expressiveness helps is weak: vendor-reported without sample sizes, gains mixed with size, placement and emphasis. Hold claims that a quieter register helps to the same standard. Usability fell where structure or labels went: keep them at any register. Whether experts tolerate more novelty is unsettled.
- Sources disagree on where expression lives (open entry areas and transitions, or system states), whether a launch or a welcome screen carries the mark, and what differentiates (meeting unmet needs, or an emotional layer). Name the side taken.

## Testing

- Run key tasks against a variant at another register, holding size, placement and emphasis constant so the result measures expression, not hierarchy.
- Check how often working users notice each expressive element, and whether that fits its moment.
- Compare intended with perceived attributes (method in `character.md`).
- Test recognition where recall matters and before changing a brand asset.

## Write down

In the direction's Visual language and Tone fields: register per screen class; each carrier's level; moments that depart from the default, which way, counted; each brand element's fate and job; the mark's place and reason; each category code kept or departed, with expected gain, cost and measure; assumptions.
